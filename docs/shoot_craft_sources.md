# Where the v2 shoot-craft vocabulary comes from

`core/shoot_style.py` encodes how fashion campaigns are actually lit, posed and shot. The
direction there is drawn from the working practice below rather than invented, and it
deliberately encodes **technique, not brand names** — see the note at the end.

## Lighting

- **Softbox key** — large (120–150cm) at ~45° to the model and slightly above eye level; clean
  skin tones with controlled shadow, the standard for lookbook and commercial work.
- **Beauty dish** — sharper definition than a softbox; resolves eye, cheekbone and collarbone
  detail. The fashion/beauty close-up standard.
- **Rim / edge light** — strip light behind, angled at hair and shoulders, roughly **1–1.5 stops
  under the key**. Creates outline separation and stops dark garments merging into the backdrop.
- **Negative fill** — black flag on the shadow side to deepen contrast and add dimension without
  moving the key. The fix for a flat-looking frame.
- **Hard/angled light reveals fabric texture; over-soft light flattens it.** Editorial lighting is
  tested against the fabric first.

## Camera

- 35–85mm covers most editorial framing. **85mm** keeps flattering proportions for portrait-range
  work; **24–35mm** adds drama and energy when the location is part of the story.
- Shallow depth of field for subject separation; the garment itself must stay sharp.

## Eyes

- The eyes are the single most important feature of a portrait — poorly lit, unfocused or
  disconnected eyes weaken the whole image.
- **Catchlights** (the reflection of the light source in the eye) are what make eyes read as alive;
  their size and shape follow the modifier used.
- Gaze direction is a deliberate choice: **to the lens** creates connection with the viewer; **away**
  reads as introspective and editorial. Chin down with eyes up lifts focus through the lashes.

## Posing

- **Contrapposto** — weight shifted onto one leg. Adds drama, defines the waist, lengthens the legs.
- S-curve through the body rather than a squared-up stance; a gap between arm and torso so the
  silhouette reads; long neck; chin slightly forward and down.
- Hands stay soft and relaxed — never clenched, never hanging dead.
- **Over-the-shoulder** turn directs attention to back detail while staying candid — the basis of
  Drape's back-view looks.

## Category specifics

- **Lingerie / intimates** — large soft directional source (big window with sheer diffusion, or a
  feathered softbox) for luminous even skin and gentle shadow; a backlight gives a glow along the
  edge. Light must skim the piece to resolve lace and trim. Hands soft, line elongated. Elegant and
  confident, never clinical.
- **Activewear** — dynamic moments (stride, lunge, reach) so the garment's performance reads.
  Punchier directional key plus rim for muscle and seam definition; flat frontal light kills
  construction. Never a ring light on synthetics — it produces circular reflections that make the
  fabric look cheap.
- **Dresses** — movement to catch drape; full or three-quarter length so the hemline reads.
- **Outerwear** — silhouette, lapel roll and shoulder line; cooler directional light keeps wool and
  leather honest.

## On brand names

Campaign references were studied for *technique*. No brand name appears in any Drape prompt.
Naming a label asks the model to imitate that house's campaign identity — a trade-dress problem —
and in practice it is also weaker direction than stating the actual setup: "large softbox at 45°,
gridded strip light behind at one stop under key" produces a more controllable result than a
brand adjective.
